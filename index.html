Your Relationship Operating System

The expectations, protective responses, and habits you fall back on when connection feels uncertain. We make them easier to notice, then practice what you want to do differently.

What we do in the room

Try the words.
Notice what happens.
Try them again.

Robert leads the session and keeps the exercise focused. You spend time practicing, with enough teaching to understand what you're trying.

You can use fictional scenarios, keep personal details private, or pass on an exercise. You do not counsel other participants.

  1. Get a short teaching

    Look at one relationship skill and the moment it can help you handle.

  2. Practice with a clear prompt

    Try an exercise with others, such as making a direct request without explaining it away.

  3. Pause for selected coaching

    Robert works with selected examples in the group. You can learn by observing; individual coaching time is not guaranteed.

  4. Reflect and choose a next step

    Name what you noticed and one response you want to keep practicing in your life.

The practice roadmap

From spotting a pattern
to making a choice.

We return to these skills in guided exercises, so you can recognize a familiar moment and have a response ready to try.

  1. 01

    Recognize your loop

    Separate what happened from the story you told yourself and the move you made next.

  2. 02

    Stay with the moment

    Notice the urge to chase, shut down, or defend. Practice taking a pause before acting.

  3. 03

    Say what you mean

    Make a clear request, name a boundary, and listen without preparing your defense.

  4. 04

    Come back and repair

    Practice returning to a difficult conversation and owning your part without taking all the blame.

What you'll work on

Something to use in
the next conversation.

The aim is to leave with language and observations that are specific to you.

  • Your pattern, written down

    A map of your trigger, the meaning you give it, and your usual response.

  • A request you can say out loud

    Words you've practiced for a need, a boundary, or a conversation you keep avoiding.

  • A repair process

    A way to return to a hard conversation, name your part, and ask what needs attention.

  • A continuing-practice plan

    A cue to watch for, one different action to try, and a way to reflect on what happened.

Before you ask

A few practical
questions.

Do I need to be in a relationship?

No. The cohort is for individuals, single or partnered. You'll work on your own responses. You can join independently of a partner.

Will I have to share personal details?

You choose what to share. You can work with fictional scenarios or pass on an exercise. Respecting others' privacy is part of participating, though no group can guarantee confidentiality.

How much individual coaching will I get?

Robert offers selected coaching within the group. A private session or individual coaching turn is not guaranteed. There is no coaching or messaging support between sessions.

Is there an app or community to keep up with?

No. The cohort centers on the live sessions. There is no app, community platform, or between-session support to manage.

Is this therapy or couples counseling?

No. RelationSync is a coaching and skills-practice cohort, not therapy, couples counseling, trauma treatment, or crisis support. If you need clinical care or help with your safety, seek a qualified professional or local emergency services.

When does it start, and what does it cost?

Dates, session schedule, and pricing will be shared before enrollment. Email Robert to ask about the cohort. An inquiry does not enroll you, reserve a seat, or commit you to a payment.
